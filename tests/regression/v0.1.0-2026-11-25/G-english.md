Coupled Sulfur–Iron Cycling Controlling Phosphorus Release from Sediments in Black-Odorous Urban Waters

**英文摘要**（Abstract）

Sediments in urban black-odorous water bodies are an important internal source of phosphorus, and phosphorus release from sediments often prevents the water-quality improvement achieved by external pollution interception from being sustained. Existing studies mainly focus on the reductive dissolution of iron-bound phosphorus under anoxic conditions, whereas how sulfide production disturbs iron cycling, and how this disturbance controls phosphorus release, has not been quantitatively understood. This project proposes that sulfide produced by sulfate reduction binds ferrous iron to form iron sulfides, which weakens the capacity of iron oxides to re-immobilize phosphorus and is the key step sustaining phosphorus release from sediments. By combining in situ microelectrode measurements with isotope tracing, the project will, first, resolve the microscale distributions of sulfide, ferrous iron and dissolved oxygen across the sediment–water interface; second, trace the coupling between sulfate reduction rates and iron speciation transformation; and finally, establish a quantitative model of phosphorus release flux under coupled sulfur–iron conditions. The project aims to elucidate how coupled sulfur–iron cycling controls phosphorus release from sediments, so as to provide a scientific basis for internal pollution control in black-odorous water bodies.
