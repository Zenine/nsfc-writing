**英文摘要**（一段，190 词）

Internal loading from sediments of black-odorous urban waters is an important source of phosphorus, and the release of sedimentary phosphorus often makes the water quality improvement achieved by external pollution interception difficult to sustain. Existing studies have mainly focused on the reductive dissolution of iron-bound phosphorus under anoxic conditions, whereas the interference of sulfide production with the iron cycle and its role in controlling phosphorus release remain poorly quantified. We hypothesize that sulfide produced by sulfate reduction binds ferrous iron to form iron sulfides, which weakens the capacity of iron oxides to re-immobilize phosphorus and is the key step sustaining phosphorus release from the sediments. This project will combine in situ microelectrode measurements with isotope tracing. First, we will resolve the microscale distributions of sulfide, ferrous iron and dissolved oxygen at the sediment–water interface. Second, we will trace the coupling between sulfate reduction rates and iron speciation transformation. Finally, we will establish a quantitative model of phosphorus release flux under coupled sulfur–iron conditions. This project aims to elucidate how coupled sulfur–iron cycling controls phosphorus release from sediments, and to provide a scientific basis for internal pollution control in black-odorous waters.
