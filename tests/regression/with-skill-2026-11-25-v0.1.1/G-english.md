## 英文摘要

Sediments in urban black-odorous water bodies are an important internal source of phosphorus, and the re-release of sediment phosphorus often makes the improvement in water quality achieved by external sewage interception difficult to sustain. Existing studies mainly focus on the reductive dissolution of iron-bound phosphorus under anoxic conditions, whereas the interference of sulfide formation with iron cycling and its role in controlling phosphorus re-release remain poorly quantified. This project proposes that sulfide produced by sulfate reduction binds ferrous iron to form iron sulfide, which weakens the capacity of iron oxides to re-immobilize phosphorus, and that this is a key step in the sustained release of sediment phosphorus. The project will combine in situ microelectrode measurements with isotope tracing. First, it will resolve the microscale distributions of sulfide, ferrous iron, and dissolved oxygen at the sediment–water interface. Second, it will trace the coupling between sulfate reduction rates and iron speciation transformation. Finally, it will establish a quantitative model of phosphorus re-release flux under coupled sulfur–iron conditions. The project aims to elucidate the mechanism by which coupled sulfur–iron cycling controls sediment phosphorus re-release, so as to provide a scientific basis for controlling internal pollution in black-odorous water bodies.
